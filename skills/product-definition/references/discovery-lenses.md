# Discovery lenses

Use this reference to find consequential branches, not to generate an exhaustive questionnaire. Select only lenses that plausibly change the product.

## User and moment

- Who experiences the problem directly, and who chooses or pays for the solution?
- What event causes them to act now?
- What are they doing immediately before and after?
- Is the problem occasional, habitual, urgent, or ambient?
- What must the user already understand or possess?

## Existing progress

- What substitute, workaround, person, or habit solves this today?
- Why would switching be worth its learning and trust cost?
- Is the real competitor another product, manual effort, or doing nothing?
- Which existing behavior should remain familiar?

## Value and boundaries

- What is the smallest outcome that creates unmistakable value?
- Which attractive capability would blur the product's identity?
- What must be fast, precise, explainable, delightful, or reversible?
- Should the product optimize for novices, frequent experts, or a transition between them?

## State and failure

- What happens when work is interrupted, stale, duplicated, partially complete, or wrong?
- What needs drafts, history, undo, approval, recovery, or auditability?
- Which state belongs to a person, team, device, organization, or external system?
- What happens when a dependency is slow or unavailable?

## Trust and power

- What can the product see, change, publish, spend, delete, or disclose?
- Which actions require preview, confirmation, permission, or notification?
- How can a malicious or careless user misuse the capability?
- What explanation does a user need before trusting an outcome?
- What data should never be collected or retained?

## Collaboration and incentives

- Who creates, reviews, approves, owns, and maintains the result?
- Can participants have conflicting goals or permissions?
- Does value increase through sharing, templates, accumulated data, or network effects?
- Who benefits while someone else bears the cost?

## Access and environments

- What changes for keyboard-only, screen-reader, low-vision, or motor-impaired users?
- What changes on small screens, poor networks, old hardware, or offline?
- Do locale, language, time zone, units, or regulation alter the workflow?

## Success and falsification

- What early behavior predicts durable value?
- What counter-metric prevents a misleading success metric?
- What observation would show that the product premise is wrong?
- How quickly can the riskiest assumption be tested without building the whole product?

## Scale and adjacency

- What breaks when usage becomes ten or one hundred times more frequent?
- Which neighboring user or workflow becomes possible if the core primitive succeeds?
- What future feature would expose a weak foundation in version one?
- Which decisions are easy to reverse, and which quietly become permanent?

## Blind-spot standard

A blind-spot question earns a place when a reasonable answer could change the target user, value proposition, core workflow, trust model, version-one scope, or viability. Interesting trivia does not qualify.
